## Activity Overview

In this activity, you will respond to a phishing incident that involves a malicious file hash. This is the same SHA256 file hash that you investigated and verified as malicious in a previous activity.

You will follow playbook instructions to investigate and resolve the incident alert ticket.

A playbook outlines the step-by-step actions required to properly respond to a security incident. Coordinated, effective, and quick action is critical during incident response.

---

## Scenario

You are a level-one security operations center (SOC) analyst at a financial services company. Previously, you received a phishing alert about a suspicious file being downloaded on an employee’s computer. After investigating the email attachment file’s hash, the attachment has already been verified as malicious.

Now you must follow your organization’s process to complete the investigation and resolve the alert.

Your organization’s security policies and procedures describe how to respond to specific alerts, including phishing incidents.

The playbook includes a flowchart and written instructions to help you complete the investigation and resolve the alert. At the end, you will update the alert ticket with your findings.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

### Step 1: Access the Template
Use the Alert ticket template for this course item.

If you do not have a Google account, download the template from the attached file.

### Step 2: Access Supporting Materials
Open the Phishing Playbook and flowchart.

Use them throughout the investigation.

The playbook provides detailed written instructions for each step in the flowchart.

The flowchart provides a high-level overview of the response process.

### Investigation Process

Before investigating the alert, review the playbook and flowchart.

In the Alert ticket template, begin by updating the Ticket status to Investigating.

Then evaluate the alert ticket and the Additional information section.

Examples of what to review:
- Alert severity
- Sender details
- Message body and subject line
- Attachments or links

After reviewing the alert, answer the 5 W’s:
- Who caused the incident?
- What happened?
- When did the incident take place?
- Where did the incident occur?
- Why did it happen?

At the end of this step, identify 2–3 reasons why the alert is or is not legitimate.

Then use the playbook’s Step 3.0 and Step 3.1 to determine whether the email contains links or attachments and whether they are malicious. In this case, the email contains an attachment whose file hash has already been verified as malicious.

Proceed to Step 3.2 if the alert should be escalated. If it should not be escalated, proceed to Step 4.

Finally, update the alert ticket in the template.

Depending on your decision:
- Set Ticket status to Closed or Escalated.
- Add Ticket comments explaining the steps taken and why you chose that resolution.

Include 2–3 reasons why you believe the ticket should be escalated or closed.

## What to Include in Your Response

Be sure to address the following points:

- Update the Ticket status column using the dropdown list
- In the Ticket comments section, provide a sentence briefly describing the alert and what happened
- In the Ticket comments section, provide 2–3 sentences describing why the ticket should be escalated or closed, using specific details from the alert

## Summary

This task reinforces the importance of following a phishing playbook during incident response. The goal is to confirm malicious activity, assess the alert, determine the proper action, and document the findings clearly in the alert ticket.
